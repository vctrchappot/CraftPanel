Owners manage users under **Settings → Users**. Give every person their own account with the lowest role that fits.

## Roles

| Role | Can |
| --- | --- |
| **Viewer** | See servers, consoles and metrics (read-only) |
| **Operator** | Everything a Viewer can, plus start, stop and restart servers, run console commands, manage players and create backups |
| **Administrator** | Everything an Operator can, plus create, configure and delete servers, manage files, plugins, worlds and schedules, restore backups, force-stop servers and read the audit log |
| **Owner** | Everything, including users and application settings |

Roles are enforced by the server on every request; the dashboard only hides what you can't use.

<Aside type="note">
Dashboard roles are separate from Minecraft's operator status. Granting `op` to a player in game requires an Administrator.
</Aside>

## Per-server access

A user either works on **all servers** with their role, optionally with a higher role on single servers, or only on **selected servers** with a role per server. Typical uses:

- A moderator who manages players on one server only.
- A friend who may restart their own creative server but nothing else.

Users limited to selected servers don't see other servers at all, and they get no application-wide rights (creating servers, settings, users, audit log).

## Two-factor sign-in

Every user can turn on two-factor sign-in with an authenticator app under **Settings → Account**. Setup shows a QR code and ten single-use recovery codes; keep them somewhere safe.

Owners can **require** two-factor sign-in per role. Users of that role must set it up at their next sign-in before they can do anything else. If someone loses their device and recovery codes, an Owner can reset their second factor.

## Signed-in devices

**Settings → Account** lists your sessions with address, browser and last activity; sign out single devices or all others. Signing in again in a browser that is already signed in replaces its session, and each account keeps at most 20 sessions; the longest-idle ones end first. Owners can sign a user out everywhere. Changing a password, a role or disabling an account ends that user's sessions immediately.
